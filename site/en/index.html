Writing cannot cost you what you are looking at.
The screen stopped being a page.

Today, while we write, we also watch, compare, test, edit, present, listen and decide. There is information happening everywhere, and yet, when we need to write, we almost always open a blank page.

Harp starts from another idea.
Writing can exist alongside what we are doing. The center is still whatever is in front of our eyes.
Writing centered on context.

Every detail starts from the same question:
how do you write without leaving what is happening?

That also changes what we expect from a writing tool.
It has to be fast enough to keep up with a thought and predictable enough never to ask for attention when it is not needed.

Harp was made for that moment.
A new relationship between writing and the screen.

What holds this up

Safe
Your text is saved on every pause, with the previous version kept. It does not depend on closing the app properly. A local history brings back what was lost.
No account
No sign-up. No cloud. Not to download it, not to get new versions.
No interruption
An overlaid window, controls that show up only when the mouse comes close, and everything by keyboard. Ctrl+Alt+Space, Win+J and Win+Alt+V work from any application.
There is always a way back
Any mode can be left in a second. Ctrl+Alt+G undoes all of them and brings the window back, even transparent or off the screen.
Real files
The text stays yours. It opens and saves .txt and .md, which any editor reads.
Control
Nothing fails quietly when it matters. If the hidden mode does not work, the warning says you do show up in the recording. A new version only installs when you say so.

In practice

  • A translucent window, no borders, always on top, opacity from 20% to 100%.
  • Glass: Win+Alt+V to write, point and circle over the screen. It copies screen and notes as one image, then leaves.
  • Drafts: Win+J over any application. Enter saves, copies and hands the focus back.
  • Ghost mode: clicks go straight through the window.
  • Out of recordings and out of screen sharing.
  • A teleprompter with continuous scrolling, in a band at the top or full screen.
  • Up to ten notes in tabs, each with an icon, and a local version history.
  • Tasks with Ctrl+Enter. Lists carry on by themselves on Enter.
  • Addresses in the text become links with Ctrl+click.
  • Corners, halves and full screen by shortcut.
  • It lives in the tray and can start with Windows, with the shortcuts already working.
  • Portuguese, English and Spanish. Light, dark or system theme, and seven accent colors.

All by keyboard
